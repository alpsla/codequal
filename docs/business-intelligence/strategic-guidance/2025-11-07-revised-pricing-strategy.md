## 💡 Pricing Strategy Rationale

### Why Go Lower Than Original Plan?

**1. Cost Structure Allows It**
```
Our cost per analysis: $0.01
Margin at $6/user (60 analyses/month): 99% margin
Even at $3/user: 98% margin

We can afford aggressive pricing.
```

**2. Market Penetration > Margin**
```
Early stage priority: Users, not profit
$6/user with 3,000 users > $12/user with 500 users
$18k MRR > $6k MRR

Get users first, raise prices later (2026).
```

**3. Competitive Displacement**
```
"50% cheaper" is a migration trigger
"75% cheaper" is irresistible for SMBs
Price = our wedge into established market
```

**4. Reduces Switching Friction**
```
$12 → $6 = "Why not try it?"
$12 → $8 = "Eh, not worth switching hassle"

Lower price = lower risk = higher conversion.
```

**5. Sets Up Future Raise**
```
2026 Price Increase Plan:
- Team: $6 → $8 (33% increase)
- Pro: $12 → $15 (25% increase)

Grandfathered users stay at $6/$12 (loyalty)
New users pay higher after we've proven value
```

## 🚨 Risks of Lower Pricing

### Risk 1: "Cheap = Low Quality" Perception
**Mitigation:**
- Emphasize technical superiority (99% auto-fix vs 40%)
- Show off V9 architecture sophistication
- Publish cost advantage analysis (why we can be cheaper)
- Lead with education angle (not just "cheap")

### Risk 2: Revenue Not Sufficient for Team
**Mitigation:**
- $6/user with 1,500 users = $9k MRR (covers 1-2 people)
- Plan for 3,000 users = $18k MRR (covers 3-4 people)
- If growth slower: Raise prices sooner (2025 not 2026)

### Risk 3: Hard to Raise Prices Later
**Mitigation:**
- Grandfather early adopters (they stay at $6)
- New users pay higher ($8-10) after 1 year
- Gradual increase ($6 → $7 → $8) over 18 months
- Communicate value additions justify increase

### Risk 4: Competitors Match Our Pricing
**Mitigation:**
- Our cost structure ($0.01) still beats theirs ($0.02-0.50)
- We can go lower if needed ($5, even $4)
- Focus on differentiation beyond price (education)
- Build brand loyalty before price wars start
